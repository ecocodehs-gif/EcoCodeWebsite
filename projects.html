Target Projects.
Pick a problem worth 14 weeks.

Every project below is scoped for a team of 3-4 students: one clear user, one measurable outcome, and nothing that needs hardware or money.

How a Project Earns a Slot

Three filters, applied before a single line of code is written.

Hyper-Local

The user is someone nearby: a neighbour, a classroom, a bus stop you actually stand at. No hypothetical personas.

Shippable in 14 Weeks

One core feature, built and deployed. No hardware, no budget, no hosting bill, nothing that needs an adult to sign a contract.

Measurable

Something you can put on a Demo Day slide: a number that moved, a wait time that dropped, a report that got answered.

This Season's Board

Food Rescue Router

Bakeries and grocery stores bin usable food every evening while shelters two kilometres away run short — and nobody knows who is free to drive.

What you'd build: a claim-a-pickup board for volunteers, with distance-sorted routes and a printable handoff slip for the receiving kitchen.

Repair Map

Repair cafés, tailors and bike co-ops already exist in the city, but they are almost impossible to find when your kettle dies.

What you'd build: a searchable map of repair options with opening hours, the items each place accepts, and a "fixed or not" note from the last visitor.

Air Quality Watch

Air quality is published per region, not per schoolyard, so students with asthma get the same warning as everyone forty kilometres away.

What you'd build: a dashboard reading open sensor APIs that raises a school-zone alert and explains the risk in plain language.

What you'd build: a neighbourhood tool library with availability windows, a deposit record, and pickup QR codes so nothing goes missing.
